Mission, Aim, Motto & Pledge

Mission

To lead young people into a saving relationship with Jesus Christ and help them embrace His call to discipleship.

Aim

The Advent message to all the world in my generation.

Motto

The love of Christ compels me.

Pledge

"Loving the Lord Jesus, I promise to take an active part in the work of the Adventist Youth Society, doing what I can to help others and to finish the work of the Gospel in all the world."

Over a Century of Youth Ministry

1852

James White publishes the first edition of The Youth's Instructor, the church's earliest dedicated outreach to young people.

Late 1800s

Local youth societies begin forming in churches across the United States, including early efforts by Luther Warren and Harry Fenner, laying the groundwork for organized youth ministry.

1907

At a meeting in Mount Vernon, Ohio, more than 100 delegates adopt the name "Seventh-day Adventist Young People's Society of Missionary Volunteers" (MV), along with an aim, motto, and pledge.

1909

The General Conference session votes to create a separate Junior Missionary Volunteer Society (JMV) for children 10 to 16, distinguishing junior ministry from senior youth work.

Mid-1900s

The senior society becomes widely known as the Adventist Youth (AY) Society, continuing to serve teens and young adults beyond Pathfinder age with its own Aim, Motto, and Pledge.

Today

Adventist Youth Ministries now links more than ten million young people worldwide through AY societies, campus ministry, young adult programs, and mission initiatives such as "I Will Go."

A Deeper Commitment

The AY Legion of Honor

Beyond the Aim, Motto, and Pledge, many AY societies invite members into a further voluntary commitment known as the Legion of Honor — a set of six personal resolutions to honor Christ in what one chooses to behold, listen to, the places one goes, one's associations, one's speech, and the care given to the body.

It reflects the heart of AY ministry: not a badge to be earned, but a daily discipleship that shapes how young people live, long after any meeting ends.
